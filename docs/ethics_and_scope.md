# Ethics and scope

This is a synthetic, non-clinical evaluation of state handling and two bounded intervention behaviours. No patient data, real help-seeking posts, medical records, diagnoses, or clinical outcome labels are collected. Examples describe fictional everyday social-evaluative stress among adults. No autonomous therapy service, deployment interface, or user-facing advice product is included.

Persistent psychological inference has particular risks: a mistaken interpretation can be reified as a personal trait, an obsolete belief can shape future responses, and a reported emotion can be confused with evidence of someone else's intent. Provenance and temporal metadata may reduce some engineering failure modes, but can be wrong themselves. The experiment tests selected detectable faults, not all harmful inference.

Real longitudinal psychological memory would contain sensitive information. Any future real-world work would need independent ethics review as appropriate, consent, clear limits on collection and retention, deletion/correction mechanisms, access controls, and qualified oversight. This package supplies none of the evidence needed to claim readiness for that use.

Synthetic latent truth is useful for control, but it is authored truth. Templates simplify culture, language, context and ambiguity. They cannot establish psychological realism or patient benefit. In particular, no observed state-management score implies therapeutic efficacy, diagnostic accuracy, or general mental-health safety. Fixed user replay cannot estimate a person's response to the generated intervention.

Automated fidelity ratings can reward plausible wording while missing an epistemic failure. They are hypotheses about text quality under a rubric, not unquestionable gold. Blinded human review is planned and has not occurred. The authoring assistant is not a clinician or human auditor; no clinician validation is claimed.

Literature informs constructs and evaluation design; it does not confer clinical validity on this ontology or rubric. Qualified psychological/expert review and a substantially different validation programme would be needed before any real-world use. Public release and preprint submission should follow independent methodological review; neither has been performed or initiated here.

## V2 additions (21 September 2026)

- The twelve V2 scenario families remain everyday, non-clinical social-evaluative situations among adults. None involves crisis, self-harm, diagnosis, medication, abuse or a clinical relationship. Appraisal text always reports the user's own interpretation and never asserts another person's intent as fact.
- **Evaluator overconfidence.** An automated judge scoring its own model family, or scoring warm wording as fidelity, can manufacture a favourable result. The primary model is not to be used as its own judge; traps now include a correct uncertainty-acknowledging response and a warm response in the wrong intervention family to measure this.
- **Inappropriate automated intervention.** Even a perfectly scored decision in this benchmark is a choice between two bounded behaviours for a synthetic user. It is not evidence that automated validation or reappraisal is appropriate for any real person.
- **Persistent inference.** V2 makes the retraction/resolution and maintenance/transition distinctions explicit, which sharpens the study's ability to detect a system treating an abandoned or never-held interpretation as current. It does not make persistent psychological memory safe.
- No human participant, rater or clinician has been involved. No deployment recommendation follows from any part of this package.
